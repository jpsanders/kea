# Review questions: <slug>

Every council seat answers these, in order, under `## N. <question>` headings. Good questions are specific and can
be scored later: "will twenty emails produce three calls in thirty days, and why" beats "is the plan good".
Six to ten questions. More and every review goes shallow.

## Standing questions

**1. Survival.** Does this plan keep things solvent through the horizon? If not, what breaks first, and when?

**2. Sequencing.** What is scheduled too early, too late, or should not be in this period at all?

**3. Capacity.** Hours claimed against hours available. Where is the plan double booked?

**4. Cash timing.** Cleared cash, not revenue. Where does the plan confuse the two?

**5. The load bearing assumption.** Which single assumption, if wrong, collapses the plan? Is it verified?

**6. What is missing.** What would a competent operator do here that this plan does not mention?

## When frameworks are named

**F1.** Is each framework applied within its boundary conditions, or cited for a conclusion it does not support?

**F2.** Which framework in the brain contradicts this plan, and is the contradiction acknowledged?

## Against flattery

If the plan is sound, say so plainly and say which parts are strongest. If it will fail, say so in the first
paragraph. Do not bury it under balanced framing.
