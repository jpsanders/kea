# Eliyahu M. Goldratt: the theory of constraints

| Field | Value |

## Core ideas

### 1. One constraint sets the pace

**Claim:** every system has one step that limits its output, and improving any other step changes nothing.

**Mechanism:** work queues in front of the slowest step; faster steps elsewhere only grow the queue.

**Decision it changes:** where to spend money and hours on speed.

**Applies here when:** the cafe or the roastery is at capacity at some hour of the day.

### 2. Exploit, subordinate, then elevate

**Claim:** first get everything out of the constraint as it is, then make every other step serve it, and only then
pay to enlarge it.

**Mechanism:** the cheap fixes at the constraint are usually large, and enlarging it moves the constraint elsewhere.

**Decision it changes:** buying equipment before rearranging the work.

**Applies here when:** a purchase is proposed to fix a slow step.

## Boundary Conditions

| This framework assumes | If that is false | Then |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Work flows through steps in sequence | Steps run in parallel, no queue | Look for the scarcest resource |
| Demand exceeds capacity at the constraint | Demand is the limit | The constraint is the market: sell |
| The constraint can be measured | It cannot be observed | Measure first; never apply it on a guess |

**The stage it assumes:** a running operation with steady demand.

**Who it does not fit:** a business still looking for demand. There the constraint is customers, not throughput.

**Known misapplications:** the grinder purchase ({{d:-45}}) assumed the grinder was the constraint without timing
it. The queue was at the till (P-0006, scored FALSE).
